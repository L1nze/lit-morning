State of charge (SOC) estimation using the extended Kalman filter (EKF) frequently suffers from error divergence due to voltage observation degradation, particularly under the coupling of high-frequency dynamic ship loads and the wide voltage plateau of large-capacity lithium iron phosphate (LFP) batteries. To address this, a joint state observation method integrating forgetting factor recursive least squares (FFRLS) and a fungi growth optimization extended Kalman filter (FGO-EKF) is proposed. Based on a dynamic second-order RC equivalent circuit model, FFRLS tracks time-varying battery parameters in real time to eliminate polarization accumulation errors. Crucially, a scaling factor is introduced into the EKF's a posteriori update phase and adaptively optimized by the FGO algorithm to rationally reshape the weight distribution of degraded voltage observations. Validated with dynamic operating data from a 280 Ah marine LFP battery, the proposed FGO-EKF method suppresses the root mean square error (RMSE) to 0.635% and constrains the maximum absolute error (MaxAbs) at 1.175%. Achieving accuracy improvements of over 54% compared to the conventional EKF, this framework effectively resolves plateau-induced estimation failures while accommodating the computational limits of shipborne battery management systems.

英文摘要

Smart battery cells have been proposed to improve the safety of lithium-ion battery packs in Battery Electric Vehicles (BEVs) and Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESSs). Powerline Communication (PLC) offers a cable-free, scalable solution for smart cell systems, enabling key battery parameters, including temperature, voltage, and current, to be transmitted through existing electrical connections. This paper proposes a large-scale series-connected cylindrical battery-based PLC channel simulation model. Simulation results for 1S1P, 5S1P, and 8S1P configurations show excellent agreement with Vector Network Analyser measurements, validating the model. The model is then extended to 50S1P, 200S1P, 400S1P, and 1200S1P, to assess the effect of scale on PLC performance. The reconstructed channel responses are used to evaluate Bit Error Rate (BER) performance under Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) conditions. The results show that 200S1P and 400S1P packs maintain reliable communication at 20 dB SNR, whereas the 1200S1P configuration suffers degradation even at 25 dB. To achieve a BER of 1 × 10 −5 , the 1200S1P, 400S1P, and 200S1P configurations require additional SNR margins of 15.751 dB, 9.602 dB, and 5.927 dB, respectively, compared with 1S1P. These findings demonstrate the feasibility of PLC for high-voltage battery systems, while highlighting the challenges of ultra-large packs for future BESSs.
